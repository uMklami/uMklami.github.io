About Me

I am a computer scientist at the Department of Computer Science, University of Oxford, working on artificial intelligence, computer vision, multimodal sensing and research software for scientific applications. I lead research software development for the MorphoCam programme, a NERC–UKRI funded collaboration between Computer Science and the Wildlife Conservation Research Unit (WildCRU), working with Prof. Andrew Markham and Dr Andrew Loveridge.

My research develops AI methods that recover physically and biologically meaningful information from wildlife imagery and multimodal sensor data: metric distance and morphology, depth, 3D structure, behaviour and individual identity.

Before Oxford, I worked on digital twins, computer vision and AR/VR at the University of Derby with industry partners including Rolls-Royce, JCB and National Gas. Alongside research, I teach Computer Science at Oxford and as a Visiting Lecturer at Coventry University. I received my PhD in Computer Science from Universiti Tun Hussein Onn Malaysia (2020).

Research

🦁

WildDepth

A multimodal RGB–LiDAR wildlife dataset and benchmark for metric depth estimation, behaviour analysis and 3D reconstruction (CVPR 2026; extended at ECCV 2026).

📏

Metric wildlife perception

A robust pipeline for estimating animal distance and height directly from camera-trap imagery, linking visual AI to ecological measurement.

🐾

Behaviour & identity

Video-based AI for behaviour analysis, including animal gait as a non-invasive signature for individual identification.

🧊

3D morphometrics

Recovering 3D shape and body measurements of animals from images using modern 3D generation and reconstruction methods.

🔁

Digital twins

Digital-twin systems integrating real-time data, analytics, 3D models and immersive visualisation for engineering and remote learning.

⚙️

Reproducible AI systems

Modular ML pipelines covering data engineering, evaluation, testing, containerisation and deployment for field-ready science.
